1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PROBLEM STATEMENT
================================================================================
Why does this project exist?
Modern weather prediction models (ECMWF, GFS, GraphCast, Pangu-Weather) produce 
dense, multi-variate numerical time series (temperatures, pressure, dew points, 
wind speeds, gusts, relative humidity, precipitation, visibility). However:
1. End-users (farmers, energy grid operators, emergency services, the general public) 
   cannot read 168 rows of hourly numbers. They need natural language explanations.
2. If you feed raw tabular data to standard Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT-4 
   or Gemini:
   - They suffer from "token-bias": LLMs get overwhelmed by noisy hourly rows and 
     confuse diurnal fluctuations with long-term persistent synoptic trends.
   - They severely hallucinate numbers: They state temperatures or rain quantities 
     that are not in the table.
   - They violate physical atmospheric laws: They assert that dry air causes rain, 
     or that dropping surface pressure occurs under high-pressure ridges.

Our Solution:
The Hierarchical AI-Meteorologist introduces:
1. Multi-scale temporal aggregation: Pre-processing raw hourly data into 6-hour and 
   daily summaries, allowing the LLM to separate intraday noise from 7-day trends.
2. A deterministic 4-Module Verification Engine: Before any LLM-generated report is 
   shown to a user, our custom Python verification layer fact-checks the numbers 
   against raw Pandas DataFrames, validates atmospheric physics rules, and checks 
   causal transitions using a directed graph.

2. BASE PAPER CONTEXT VS. OUR OWN INNOVATION
================================================================================
The Base Paper:
- Title: "Hierarchical AI-Meteorologist: LLM-Agent System for Multi-Scale and 
  Explainable Weather Forecast Reporting"
- Authors: Daniil Sukhorukov, Andrei Zakharov, Nikita Glazkov, et al.
- Published: arXiv:2511.23387v1 [cs.AI], 28 November 2025.

What the Base Paper Provided:
- The 3-Block architecture concept: Assistant -> Meteorologist -> Writer.
- The concept of multi-scale temporal aggregation (hourly, 6-hour, daily).
- Textual generation of a "summary", "proof block", and "keywords".

Where the Base Paper Stopped (Their Explicit Limitations):
In Section 3.7 ("Current Limitations") and Section 5 ("Future Work"), the authors 
openly admitted:
"The system does not perform separate 'tabular reasoning' with programmatic 
hypothesis testing; verifiability is realized through the two-step textual 
rationale... It can be further improved by complementing the existing text-based 
verification with programmatic checks and localizing keyword thresholds to improve 
tropical and regional sensitivity."

What WE Built (Our Novel Engineering Contributions):
1. Programmatic 4-Module Verification Engine:
   We built the missing verification engine in Python:
   - DataFactValidator: Directly checks claims against Pandas DataFrames.
   - RuleEngine: Evaluates 16 atmospheric physics laws with time lags and margins.
   - CausalGraph: NetworkX DAG detecting forbidden transitions (e.g. dry air -> rain).
   - TemporalChecker: Catches directional contradictions between hourly and daily claims.
   - ConfidenceScorer: Combines modules using a weighted harmonic mean with zero-guards.
2. Universal Climatological Anomaly Verification:
   The base paper suffered from false alarms in tropical cities (Manila) because it 
   used static temperature thresholds. We engineered universal anomaly verification 
   that dynamically evaluates forecasts against 30-year station normals (Delta T >= +2°C).
3. Closed-Loop Critic-Corrector Loop:
   When verification fails, the system compiles a structured critique and sends it 
   back to the LLM to self-correct its errors.
4. Empirical Evaluation Benchmark:
   Benchmarked over 200 runs across 53 Indian climate zones, recording to Supabase.

4. PHASE 1: DATA INGESTION & GEOSPATIAL PROCESSING (BLOCK 1)
================================================================================
File: app/assistant/context_builder.py, geocoding.py, climatology.py, openmeteo.py

Step 1.1: Location Resolution (geocoding.py)
- Resolves city name, latitude, longitude, country, and administrative region.
- Queries GeoNames ASTER GDEM API for exact terrain elevation above sea level (meters).
- Queries Wikipedia API for concise historical/geographic summary (falls back 
  gracefully with WIKIPEDIA_UNAVAILABLE degradation code if unreachable).

Step 1.2: Historical Climatology Retrieval (climatology.py)
- Queries Meteostat Python package for 30-year climate normals (1991–2020 WMO standard).
- Station lookup: Finds nearest physical meteorological station.
- Extracts monthly normals: {month: 1..12, tmax_c, tmin_c, ptot_mm}.
- Fallback System: If Meteostat station data is missing, automatically falls back to 
  Open-Meteo ERA5 reanalysis archive (0.25° grid, 1991–2020 daily aggregation).

Step 1.3: Forecast Data Harvesting (openmeteo.py)
- Ingests high-resolution forecast data from Open-Meteo:
  * Current snapshot: Real-time temperature, humidity, wind, pressure, visibility.
  * Hourly series (168 hours / 7 days): t_c, t_feel_c, td_c, rh_percent, wind_speed_ms, 
    wind_direction_deg, wind_gust_ms, precipitation_mm, visibility_m, pressure_hpa.
  * Daily forecast: daily max/min temperatures, precipitation sums, max wind speeds.

Step 1.4: NWS Area Forecast Discussion (nws.py)
- For United States locations, retrieves NOAA National Weather Service expert human 
  discussions as a weak consistency baseline. Skipped for international locations.

5. PHASE 2: HIERARCHICAL TEMPORAL AGGREGATION & FORMULAS
================================================================================
File: app/assistant/aggregation.py

Why Aggregation is Critical:
Flat hourly tables contain 168 rows of minute noise. The LLM cannot distinguish 
between an afternoon convective shower and an incoming synoptic frontal system. 
We pre-aggregate data over non-overlapping windows W in {6h, 1d}:

1. Mathematical Means, Extremes, and Sums:
   - Mean Temperature:       T_mean = (1 / |W|) * sum(T_t)
   - Max Temperature:        T_max  = max(T_t)
   - Min Temperature:        T_min  = min(T_t)
   - Mean Relative Humidity: RH_mean = (1 / |W|) * sum(RH_t)
   - Mean Wind Speed:        U_mean = (1 / |W|) * sum(U_t)
   - Total Precipitation:    P_sum  = sum(P_t)

2. Circular Wind Averaging (atan2 Vector Trigonometry):
   Standard scalar averaging of angles is mathematically invalid:
   Example: Winds from 350° (NNW) and 10° (NNE)
   Scalar average = (350 + 10) / 2 = 180° (South - completely wrong!).
   We project angles onto the trigonometric unit circle:
     Y = (1 / |W|) * sum(sin(rad(theta_t)))
     X = (1 / |W|) * sum(cos(rad(theta_t)))
     theta_mean = atan2(Y, X) [converted to degrees modulo 360]
   * Circular mean is retained for 6-hour windows and omitted for daily windows to 
     prevent misleading circular distortion across diurnal wind reversals.

3. Dynamic Context Mode Pruning:
   - Lead Time < 7 Days: Full Hierarchical Mode (Metadata + Climatology + Daily + 
     6-Hour + Hourly series included).
   - Lead Time >= 7 Days: Lite Hierarchical Mode (Hourly series dropped to save 60% 
     of input tokens, relying exclusively on 6h and daily aggregates).

6. PHASE 3: METEOROLOGICAL REASONING AGENT (BLOCK 2)
================================================================================
File: app/llm/meteorologist.py, prompts.py

The LLM is prompted strictly as an atmospheric diagnostic expert. It receives the 
compact JSON context and must return a strictly formatted JSON object:
- "summary": 1-2 paragraphs detailing causal atmospheric dynamics.
- "proof": 6-8 bullet points quoting specific numerical aggregates.
- "keywords": 3-5 concise meteorological descriptors (e.g. "cooling trend", "heavy rain").
- "warnings": Hazardous condition alerts (omitted if conditions are benign).
- "claims": Array of up to 8 structured, verifiable weather claims:
  Format: {
    "claim_id": "CLAIM_001",
    "variable": "t_max_c",
    "assertion_type": "threshold",
    "window_start_utc": "2026-10-04T00:00:00Z",
    "window_end_utc": "2026-10-04T23:59:59Z",
    "threshold_or_delta": "> 34.0",
    "data_scale": "daily"
  }
- "causal_chain": Array of 4-8 chronological transition states selected from a 
  controlled vocabulary: [pressure_drop, pressure_rise, stable_pressure, wind_increase, 
  light_winds, wind_gusts, high_humidity, dry_air, rainfall, no_rain, warming, cooling, 
  extreme_heat, low_visibility, fog_persistence, thunderstorm].

7. PHASE 4: THE 4-MODULE PROGRAMMATIC VERIFICATION ENGINE
================================================================================
Directory: app/verification/

This is the core differentiator and technical novelty of the project. Every generated 
response is audited across four independent modules:

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 1: DATA FACT VALIDATOR (data_validator.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Verifies whether the numbers and trends asserted in the LLM's claims actually 
exist in the underlying observational DataFrames.

Execution Steps:
1. Maps user variable names to exact DataFrame columns across scales (hourly, six_hour, 
   daily, climatology, current).
2. Filters DataFrame rows to match the claim's UTC time window.
3. Evaluates assertion types:
   - Trend Assertions (Increasing / Decreasing):
     * Computes linear regression slope: slope = (N*sum(i*x) - sum(i)*sum(x)) / (N*sum(i^2) - (sum(i))^2)
     * Computes monotonic step ratio: fraction of consecutive steps delta_i = x_{i+1} - x_i 
       matching the claimed direction.
     * Passes if and only if:
       Increasing: slope > 0.001 AND monotonic ratio >= 0.50
       Decreasing: slope < -0.001 AND monotonic ratio >= 0.50
   - Threshold Assertions (e.g. "> 25.0", "<= 10.0"):
     * Computes exceedance confidence: fraction of observations satisfying condition.
     * Passes if confidence >= 0.25 (at least 25% of window meets condition).
   - Category Assertions (e.g. "heavy precipitation", "strong wind"):
     * Maps categorical names to numeric boundaries using thresholds.yaml.
     * Passes if confidence >= 0.50.
   - Temperature Delta (Robust Change Estimate):
     * For N >= 3: delta_T = mean(last_third) - mean(first_third).
     * For N < 3: delta_T = x_N - x_1.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 2: METEOROLOGICAL RULE ENGINE (rule_engine.py & met_rules.yaml)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Validates that cause-and-effect relationships comply with 16 physical laws 
of atmospheric thermodynamics.

The 16 Rules in Database:
- RULE_001 (Baric Wind): Pressure drop <= -3.0 hPa in 6h ==> Wind speed >= 8.0 m/s (3h lag).
- RULE_002 (Saturation Precipitation): RH > 90% ==> Precipitation > 0 mm (0h lag).
- RULE_003 (Thermal Low): Temperature > 35°C ==> Pressure drop < -1.5 hPa (2h lag).
- RULE_004 (Thunderstorm Convection): Weather code thunderstorm ==> Rain >= 7.6 mm.
- RULE_005 (Cold Front Passage): Temp drop < -3°C in 3h ==> Pressure rise > 1.5 hPa (1h lag).
- RULE_006 (Sea Breeze Initiation): Temp > 28°C ==> Wind speed > 4.0 m/s (1h lag).
- RULE_007 (Cyclone Wind Risk): Pressure drop <= -6.0 hPa in 6h ==> Wind >= 17.2 m/s (Gale 8).
- RULE_008 (Dry Air Rain Suppression): RH < 30% ==> Rain = 0 mm (Virga evaporation).
- RULE_009 (Warm Front Advection): Temp rise > 3°C in 6h ==> Pressure drop < -1.5 hPa.
- RULE_010 (Radiational Cooling): RH < 40% ==> Night temp drop < -4.0°C in 6h.
- RULE_011 (Radiation Fog Formation): RH >= 98% ==> Visibility < 1000m (WMO definition).
- RULE_015 (High Pressure Suppression): Pressure > 1025 hPa ==> Wind <= 3.4 m/s (Light).
- RULE_016 (Fog Persistence): RH > 90% + Stable Pressure + Light Wind ==> Fog persists.

Engineering Protections Against False Flags:
1. 50% Magnitude Guard: Numeric rules require claim value to reach at least 50% of 
   threshold before firing (stops trivial 0.1 hPa noise from firing storm rules).
2. Measurement Margins (epsilon): Temp +/-1.0°C, RH +/-5%, Pressure +/-2.0 hPa, Rain +/-0.5mm.
3. Temporal Lag Windows: Consequent claim must appear within [L - 3h, L + 6h].
4. Dual-Path Firing: Evaluates via both Claims list and Causal Chain.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 3: CAUSAL GRAPH ENGINE (causal_graph.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Verifies the chronological validity of atmospheric transitions using graph theory.

How it works:
1. Builds a directed graph (networkx.DiGraph) linking atmospheric states with 14 system edges.
2. Normalizes synonyms (e.g. "humidity_rise" -> "high_humidity", "clearing" -> "no_rain").
3. For each adjacent transition (u -> v) in the LLM's causal chain:
   - Direct edge: Uses edge confidence weight.
   - Indirect path: Computes cumulative path confidence with path-length penalty:
     score = (1 / k) * product(confidence_j).
4. Strictly Enforces 5 Forbidden Transitions (Physically Impossible Physics):
   - dry_air -> rainfall (rain cannot form from dry air)
   - dry_air -> fog_formation
   - dry_air -> fog_persistence
   - cooling -> extreme_heat
   - clearing -> rainfall
   * ZERO-OUT RULE: If ANY forbidden edge appears, the entire causal validity score 
     is collapsed strictly to 0.0.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODULE 4: TEMPORAL CONSISTENCY CHECKER (temporal_checker.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Catches cross-scale directional contradictions across time horizons.

How it works:
1. Separates claims by scale: daily, six_hour, hourly.
2. Identifies pairs of claims referencing the same variable with overlapping time windows.
3. Extracts directional intent (up / down / neutral).
4. Contradiction Flag: If a daily claim asserts "temperature increasing" while an hourly 
   claim asserts "temperature decreasing" within the same window, it flags a HIGH-SEVERITY 
   contradiction and applies penalty score:
   consistency_score = max(0.0, 1.0 - (sum(penalties) / checks_count)).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONFIDENCE SCORER (confidence_scorer.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mathematical Formulation:
The three core physical check modules—Data Fact (S_f), Causal Graph (S_c), and 
Temporal Consistency (S_t)—are combined using a WEIGHTED HARMONIC MEAN:

   S_base = (w_f + w_c + w_t) / [ (w_f / S_f) + (w_c / S_c) + (w_t / S_t) ]

Weights:
   w_f = 0.35 (Data Fact Weight)
   w_c = 0.20 (Causal Graph Weight)
   w_t = 0.20 (Temporal Consistency Weight)
   w_r = 0.10 (Meteorological Rule Weight, added additively)

The Harmonic Mean Zero-Guard:
In arithmetic means, an AI could get 100% on grammar and 0% on factual numbers and 
still get an 80% passing grade. In our weighted harmonic mean:
   If ANY of S_f, S_c, or S_t is 0.0 ===> S_base collapses STRICTLY to 0.0.
A fatal physical hallucination cannot be masked by good formatting.

Overall Score:
   Overall_Score = [ S_base * (w_f + w_c + w_t) + S_r * w_r ] / [ (w_f + w_c + w_t) + w_r ]
Passing Threshold: 0.55 (default).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CRITIC-CORRECTOR LOOP & EVALUATION TRACKING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- When Overall_Score < 0.55, the system compiles a structured critique:
  "[HIGH] Component: causal_graph - Forbidden edge dry_air -> rainfall detected."
  It appends "FEEDBACK ON PREVIOUS ATTEMPT" to the prompt and re-prompts Gemini to self-heal.
- EvaluationTracker (evaluator.py):
  Logs False Claim Rate (FCR), Hallucination Precision, Hallucination Recall, and Verified 
  Report Rates to .cache/evaluation_log.json across all batch runs.

8. PHASE 5: UNIVERSAL CLIMATOLOGICAL ANOMALY VERIFICATION (THE MANILA FIX)
================================================================================
The Known Research Paper Flaw:
In Section 4, the base paper tested Manila, Philippines. The forecast temperature 
was 30.5°C. The LLM generated the keyword "warm_anomaly".
Why was this wrong? In Manila in October, the 30-year normal high is 31.5°C!
A temperature of 30.5°C is actually slightly cooler than average. The model made a 
false alarm because standard systems use static mid-latitude rules (> 28°C = hot).

Our Universal Solution:
We implemented dynamic climatological anomaly verification that works universally 
for any city on Earth:
1. thresholds.yaml: Defines anomaly deviation thresholds relative to monthly normals:
   climatological_anomaly:
     warm_anomaly_delta_c: 2.0   (Forecast max >= Normal max + 2.0°C)
     cold_anomaly_delta_c: -2.0  (Forecast min <= Normal min - 2.0°C)
2. prompts.py: Injects the active forecast month's 30-year station normals into the prompt:
   "CLIMATOLOGICAL NORMALS: Month 10: Normal High=31.5°C, Normal Low=24.5°C.
    ANOMALY RULE: Only assert 'warm anomaly' if highs exceed 33.5°C."
3. data_validator.py: Intercepts any claim asserting an anomaly and calculates:
   Delta_T = Forecast_Max - Climatology_Normal_Max
   If Delta_T < 2.0°C, the claim FAILS with an explicit reason:
   "Warm anomaly verification: forecast max is 30.5°C, historical normal max is 31.5°C 
    (delta: -1.0°C, required: >=+2.0°C)."

10. PHASE 7: RESTFUL MICROSERVICE API & RELIABILITY
================================================================================
File: app/main.py, cache_store.py, degradation.py

Endpoints:
- POST /api/pipeline: Full one-shot execution (Assistant -> Meteorologist -> Writer).
- POST /context: Executes Block 1 only (returns serialized ContextPayload).
- POST /analysis: Executes Block 2 only (returns raw meteorologist JSON + verification score).
- POST /report: Executes Block 2 + 3 on pre-built context.
- GET /api/evaluation: Aggregated accuracy metrics across all recorded batch runs.
- GET /: Interactive single-page web UI dashboard.

Deterministic Caching (cache_store.py):
- Inputs are serialized into canonical JSON and hashed using SHA-256.
- Cached under .cache/{namespace}/{sha256_hash}.json.
- Identical requests skip API calls, returning instant responses with CACHE_HIT degradation codes.

Graceful Degradation:
- If Meteostat is unavailable: Falls back to ERA5 archive (ERA5_FALLBACK).
- If Wikipedia returns 403: Continues with WIKIPEDIA_UNAVAILABLE without crashing.
- Secrets scrubbing: API keys are redacted from error traces before reaching the client.

12. VIVA / DEFENSE Q&A PREPARATION CHEAT SHEET
================================================================================
Q1: "Did you train your own AI model?"
A: "No. We utilized Google Gemini as the core reasoning engine, but our primary engineering contribution is the multi-scale temporal data pipeline and the custom 4-module deterministic verification engine that fact-checks the model's output against raw data and atmospheric physics."

Q2: "Why can't you just ask the LLM to 'be truthful' in the prompt?"
A: "Prompt engineering is stochastic and cannot provide mathematical guarantees. In high-stakes domains like meteorology or disaster response, an AI must be bounded by deterministic software guardrails. Our Python engine intercepts the claims and verifies them against raw DataFrames before the user ever sees them."

Q3: "Why did you use circular averaging for wind direction instead of regular mean?"
A: "Compass angles are circular, defined on [0°, 360°). If wind blows from 350° (NNW) and 10° (NNE), a regular arithmetic average gives 180° (South), which is 180 degrees backwards! We convert angles into unit sine and cosine vectors and use atan2(sum_sin, sum_cos) to get 0° (North)."

Q4: "Why use a harmonic mean for confidence scoring instead of an arithmetic average?"
A: "An arithmetic average allows a high score in one area to hide a critical failure in another. If an LLM writes beautiful English but claims it will rain from dry air (a physical impossibility), an arithmetic mean would still give it a passing 80%. Our weighted harmonic mean features a zero-guard: if any core physical check is 0.0, the overall score collapses strictly to 0.0."

Q5: "What was the Manila limitation in the base paper, and how did you fix it?"
A: "The base paper used static temperature cutoffs (> 28°C), causing false 'warm anomaly' alerts in tropical cities like Manila where 30°C is normal. We fixed this by introducing Climatologically-Relative Anomaly Verification: we condition the model on 30-year station normals and mathematically verify that an anomaly claim only passes if Delta T >= +2.0°C above the location's historical monthly normal."
